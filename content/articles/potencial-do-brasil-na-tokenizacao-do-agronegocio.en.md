In recent years, blockchain technology has moved beyond cryptocurrencies and opened up a new frontier in financial markets: Real World Assets (RWA), or tokenized real-world assets. The concept involves representing physical assets, such as real estate, commodities or debt securities, as digital tokens recorded on a blockchain.

Against this backdrop, a particularly relevant application for Brazil is emerging, and it is not one that immediately comes to mind: the tokenization of cattle. The idea is to turn animals in a herd into tradable digital assets, creating new avenues for financing, liquidity and market access for farmers and investors alike.

## What RWAs are and why they matter

Real World Assets (RWA) are tangible or financial assets digitized on a blockchain. Each token represents a fraction of, or the whole of, a physical asset, allowing it to be traded more efficiently, transparently and programmably, and with far greater liquidity thanks to blockchain networks that never stop running.

According to Investax Research, adoption of real-world asset tokenization is growing fast. The total value of RWAs recorded on blockchains rose from roughly US$2.9 billion in 2022 to more than US$30 billion in 2025, an increase of over 900% in just three years.

The potential, however, goes well beyond this early growth. Analysts also project that tokenization could become a multitrillion-dollar market over the next decade, with estimates pointing to around US$16 trillion in tokenized assets by 2030, according to a study by Boston Consulting Group, and as much as US$30 trillion under more aggressive adoption scenarios.

The key innovation lies in the programmability and transparency of blockchain. Through smart contracts, assets can be traded, fractionalized and settled automatically, cutting operating costs and eliminating intermediaries.

Assets already being tokenized include:

- debt securities
- real estate
- commodities
- carbon credits
- financial receivables
- agricultural products

In this landscape, agribusiness stands out as one of the sectors with the greatest potential for transformation.

## Cattle tokenization: how it works

Cattle tokenization means registering each animal, or each lot of animals, as a digital asset on the blockchain. Each token represents an economic right tied to the animal, which may include a share in the cattle's appreciation or in the revenue from its sale.

The process typically involves:

1. **Registering the physical asset.** Each animal is identified through traceability systems such as microchips, electronic ear tags or health records.

2. **Custody and auditing.** An entity guarantees that the animals exist and are being properly managed.

3. **Issuing the token.** A smart contract issues tokens that represent that asset on the blockchain.

4. **Trading and liquidity.** Investors can buy fractions of the asset, financing rural production and gaining exposure to the potential returns of this market.

This model creates a new way to finance agribusiness, allowing producers to raise capital directly in the digital market, with greater liquidity than traditional models such as cattle auctions or conventional rural credit.

## Brazil's potential

Brazil has one of the largest cattle herds in the world. According to the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE), the country has more than 230 million head of cattle, a figure that even exceeds Brazil's own population, and it is also the world's leading exporter of beef.

This points to enormous potential for tokenizing livestock assets in the country.

Among the main factors that give Brazil an edge in this market:

### 1. Scale of agribusiness

Agribusiness accounts for about 25% of Brazil's GDP and is one of the most globally competitive sectors in the economy.

### 2. Demand for financing

Small and mid-sized producers often struggle to access traditional credit. Tokenization could create new channels of decentralized financing, connecting producers directly with investors.

### 3. Advances in traceability

Animal tracking and health certification technologies are already widespread in the sector, making it easier to link physical assets to digital tokens.

### 4. Global market

International investors could invest directly in Brazilian farm and livestock production through tokens, boosting the sector's liquidity and democratizing access to the returns of this market.

## Benefits of tokenization in agribusiness

Applying RWAs to cattle ranching could bring several structural benefits:

1. **Liquidity for traditionally illiquid assets.** Livestock and agricultural output are not usually traded directly in financial markets. Tokenization allows them to be traded 24 hours a day, seven days a week, with few geographic barriers.

2. **Fractional investment.** Investors can buy small fractions of a livestock asset, democratizing access to agribusiness investment.

3. **Transparency and traceability.** Blockchain makes it possible to record the entire production chain, building trust among producers, investors and consumers.

4. **Operational efficiency.** Smart contracts can automate payments, the distribution of returns and the settlement of assets.

## Regulatory and operational challenges

For all its potential, real-world asset tokenization still faces significant challenges.

Among them:

- **Financial regulation.** Determining how tokens tied to physical assets should be classified legally. Even so, Brazil is often cited as one of the countries that has made the most progress in the regulatory debate over blockchain in the financial system.

- **Custody of the physical asset.** Ensuring that the asset actually exists and is protected, which requires regulators and reliable auditing mechanisms.

- **Data standardization.** Integrating traditional farm and livestock systems with blockchain platforms.

In Brazil, bodies such as the Comissão de Valores Mobiliários (CVM, Brazil's securities regulator) have already begun discussing the tokenization of financial assets, which could pave the way for broader applications in agribusiness.

## The future of RWAs in agribusiness

The convergence of blockchain, decentralized finance (DeFi) and agribusiness could profoundly transform the way agricultural assets are financed and traded, not only in Brazil but around the world.

If implemented properly, the tokenization model could:

- broaden producers' access to capital
- connect Brazilian agribusiness to global capital
- increase transparency across the production chain
- create new digital markets for agricultural commodities

Given the size of its agribusiness sector and its global importance in food production, Brazil has the potential to become one of the world's leading hubs for agriculture-linked RWAs.
